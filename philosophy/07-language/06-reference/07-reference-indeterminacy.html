Reference Indeterminacy

Suppose every observation, assent, behavioural disposition, and theoretical sentence in a community is known. Does that evidence uniquely determine what its words refer to? Quine, Putnam, and related arguments suggest it may not. Systematic reinterpretations can preserve all observable and formal relations while assigning different objects as referents.

Reference indeterminacy is stronger than ordinary uncertainty caused by incomplete evidence. The claim is that no further fact of the relevant kind selects one assignment over empirically equivalent alternatives. The conclusion ranges from modest underdetermination of translation to global inscrutability of reference.

The arguments force a metasemantic choice. Reference may be fixed by causal history, naturalness, rational interpretation, communal use, or primitive word-world relations. If every candidate is itself representable inside the theory being permuted, the indeterminacy can reappear at a higher level.

Radical translation

Quine imagines a linguist encountering an unfamiliar language without a bilingual manual. A native speaker says:

Gavagai!

when a rabbit appears. The evidence supports translating "gavagai" as rabbit. But it also appears compatible with:

  • undetached rabbit part;
  • temporal stage of a rabbit;
  • rabbit fusion;
  • manifestation of rabbithood; or
  • something systematically related to rabbits.

Pointing does not settle the matter. The same stimulus prompts assent under each translation, provided surrounding expressions and identity criteria are adjusted systematically.

The example begins with field linguistics but targets a philosophical assumption: behavioural evidence may determine patterns of use without uniquely decomposing them into familiar objects and reference relations.

Stimulus meaning

Quine grounds translation initially in dispositions to assent or dissent under sensory stimulation. A sentence's stimulus meaning is roughly the range of stimulations that would prompt assent or dissent.

Observation sentences shared under similar circumstances provide a foothold. Translation then extends through logical particles, theoretical sentences, and connections across the speaker's total web of belief.

Stimulus meaning is too coarse to determine reference. Different ontologies can produce the same assent patterns. It also abstracts from internal structure: agreement on whole sentences need not uniquely determine which subexpression refers to which component of the world.

Quine's holism makes this expected. Translation is evaluated globally, not word by word.

Indeterminacy versus underdetermination

Underdetermination says available evidence supports more than one theory, although one may still be correct because of facts not captured by the evidence.

Indeterminacy says there is no further fact selecting among equally adequate translation manuals. The alternatives are not rival hypotheses about a hidden semantic reality; they are different schemes for organizing the same dispositions and theory.

The step from underdetermination to indeterminacy requires argument. A realist can accept that translators lack evidence while maintaining that causal history, intentions, natural properties, or primitive reference facts make one manual correct.

Quine's naturalism resists such facts if they make no difference within empirical theory. A non-naturalist need not accept that constraint.

Inscrutability of reference

Reference becomes inscrutable when systematic transformations preserve the truth-values and inferential structure of an entire theory while changing what terms refer to.

Suppose a function π maps each object in a domain to another object. Interpret each name a as π(a) and transform predicate extensions accordingly:

[[P]]′={π(x):x∈[[P]]}.

For a suitable permutation, every sentence retains its truth value. The formal theory cannot distinguish the original interpretation from the permuted one.

If all evidence is expressible through those sentences, reference assignments appear underdetermined. One manual says "rabbit" refers to rabbits; another says it refers to their proxies, with every predicate adjusted to compensate.

Proxy functions

Quine's proxy-function argument maps objects to systematic proxies, such as their singleton sets or space-time complements. Quantification and predication can be reinterpreted so that total theory remains true.

The point is not that ordinary speakers secretly refer to singleton rabbits. It is that reference may be relative to a translation or coordinate scheme. What stays invariant is global structure rather than object-by-object pairing.

A critic can say the proxy manual is less natural, more complex, or causally inappropriate. Quine asks whether these responses identify objective reference facts or merely express preferences internal to our home scheme.

Putnam's model-theoretic argument

Putnam argues that even an ideal total theory can have multiple models. If the theory is consistent and sufficiently expressive, model-theoretic results and permutation constructions allow interpretations that satisfy all its sentences while assigning unexpected referents.

The rough structure is:

  1. Let T contain every theoretical and observational sentence accepted under ideal conditions.
  2. T has a model M.
  3. Systematic reinterpretation yields other models satisfying T.
  4. Nothing stated inside T selects one as intended.
  5. Therefore truth defined merely as satisfaction in an intended model presupposes the very reference relation it was meant to explain.

The argument targets metaphysical realism understood as a fixed, theory-independent correspondence between language and a ready-made world.

It does not follow simply from the existence of non-isomorphic models that natural language is indeterminate. The crucial premise is that every admissible constraint on reference can be represented within the theory and permuted with it.

The metalanguage response

One can say:

"Rabbit" refers to rabbits, not to singleton rabbits.

But the argument can ascend. What fixes "rabbit" in the metalanguage? If a further metatheory states the constraint, that theory too admits reinterpretation.

The regress resembles the object-language/metalanguage hierarchy described in Models, Truth, and Metalanguage. Formal semantics always operates from an interpreted metalanguage; it does not generate its own intended interpretation.

This is not a contradiction. It shows that model-theoretic satisfaction cannot by itself be the full metasemantics. A defender must ground the metalanguage through use, causal contact, naturalness, or a primitive relation.

Causal constraints

A causal theorist replies that words refer to the objects causally responsible for their use through appropriate historical chains. Rabbits, not singleton sets of rabbits, cause ordinary "rabbit" tokens.

The indeterminist can transform causal predicates too, but this may appear to miss the explanatory role of real causal interaction. Reference is not constrained only by which sentences are true; it is embedded in perception, action, learning, and intervention.

Problems remain:

  • causes are numerous and grain-sensitive;
  • deviant causal chains can connect the wrong object;
  • abstract terms lack simple causal sources;
  • causal descriptions require interpreted vocabulary; and
  • a permutation can preserve relational structure.

Causation narrows eligible interpretations without transparently selecting one in every case.

Naturalness and eligibility

Lewis and other realists appeal to natural properties or eligibility. Interpretations assigning reasonably simple, joint-carving properties are better than gerrymandered alternatives.

"Rabbit" refers to rabbits because rabbithood is more natural than a disjunctive or proxy property. Overall interpretation balances:

  • fit with use;
  • simplicity;
  • naturalness of assigned properties;
  • compositional coherence; and
  • explanatory integration.

This blocks bizarre permutation manuals if naturalness is objective.

The cost is substantive metaphysics. The account presupposes privileged joints in reality and must explain how naturalness applies to social, mathematical, normative, and context-sensitive vocabulary.

Davidsonian charity

Davidson approaches interpretation through speakers' attitudes and shared environment. A correct interpretation should make speakers broadly coherent, largely right about salient surroundings, and responsive to common causes.

Triangulation among speaker, interpreter, and shared object constrains reference. The object causing convergent responses anchors their words.

Charity does not require maximal agreement about every claim. It supplies the background needed to identify disagreement as disagreement about a common topic.

This reduces radical indeterminacy by embedding reference in rational and causal interpretation. Multiple equally good total interpretations may remain, but Davidson can treat differences lacking explanatory consequences as no genuine semantic difference.

Speaker intention and community practice

Intentions can constrain which object a speaker means, while community practice constrains which intentions are linguistically effective. Historical transmission and correction distinguish inherited reference from arbitrary private assignment.

These resources are resistant to a purely formal permutation because they include:

  • embodied attention;
  • practical success and failure;
  • deference;
  • correction;
  • ostension;
  • intervention; and
  • continuity through time.

The sceptic asks whether describing these factors already assumes determinate reference. The realist answers that metasemantic explanation can be holistic without being viciously circular: causal, intentional, and semantic facts may be mutually constrained rather than reduced in one direction.

Structuralism about reference

One response accepts that object-level reference is not absolute. What is determinate is structural position: patterns of relations, inference, and interaction.

If two interpretations are related by an isomorphism preserving all relevant structure, there may be no substantive question which object assignment is "really" correct. Reference functions like coordinates: different assignments represent the same invariant structure.

Structuralism is attractive in mathematics and physics, where objects can be individuated by relational role. It is less obviously adequate for demonstrative contact with a particular object or historically grounded names.

A moderate view allows structural equivalence in some domains and determinate objectual reference in others.

Ontological relativity

Quine generalizes inscrutability to ontological relativity. Questions about which objects a theory quantifies over are answerable only relative to a background translation manual.

This does not mean any interpretation is as good as any other. Manuals must preserve the total pattern of assent, inference, prediction, and action. The claim is that several manuals can satisfy all those constraints.

The ontological consequences are developed in Quantifier Variance and Deflationism. The semantic argument should be assessed before assuming that a preferred ontology can simply select the intended interpretation.

Local versus global indeterminacy

Global permutation arguments can distract from ordinary local indeterminacy. Reference may be unsettled because:

  • a baptism was ambiguous;
  • a historical chain split;
  • expert communities disagree;
  • a term is vague or polysemous;
  • a fictional or theoretical posit failed;
  • context does not identify one demonstratum; or
  • a classification is undergoing change.

These cases do not require the conclusion that all reference is inscrutable. They show that determinacy can vary by expression, context, and explanatory purpose.

A theory should distinguish:

  • exact unique reference;
  • partial or disjunctive reference;
  • reference fixed only relative to a scheme;
  • genuine ambiguity;
  • vagueness; and
  • complete failure.

What survives the indeterminacy arguments

The arguments establish several durable lessons:

  1. Formal satisfaction alone does not select an intended interpretation.
  2. Behavioural evidence may determine less than object-by-object reference.
  3. Reference claims require a metasemantics, not merely a model.
  4. Causal, social, intentional, and naturalness constraints must be stated rather than assumed.
  5. Some differences among interpretation manuals may be representational rather than factual.
  6. Determinacy should be argued locally rather than presumed globally.

They do not straightforwardly establish that words refer to nothing, that all translations are equal, or that ordinary reference is an illusion. The live dispute is whether the best total explanation uniquely selects referents, selects them only up to structural equivalence, or leaves no fact beyond equally good interpretive schemes.
