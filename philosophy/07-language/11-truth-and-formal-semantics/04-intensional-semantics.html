Intensional Semantics

Extensional semantics assigns objects, sets, and truth values. It cannot by itself explain modality, tense, attitude reports, or expressions whose extension varies across circumstances. Intensional semantics assigns functions from indices - commonly worlds and times - to extensions.

This page presents the formal architecture compactly. The philosophical interpretation and limits are developed in Intensionality and Possible-Worlds Content.

Types

Let:

  • e be individuals;
  • t truth values;
  • s worlds.

Then:

  • name: e;
  • predicate: ⟨e,t⟩;
  • proposition/intension: ⟨s,t⟩;
  • intensional predicate: ⟨s,⟨e,t⟩⟩.

Functional application composes matching types.

Intension

For sentence p:

[[p]]=λw.[[p]]w.

This function corresponds to the set of worlds where p is true.

For a predicate:

[[red]]=λw.λx.redw​(x).

Its extension can vary by world.

Modality

With accessibility relation R:

[[□p]]w=1iff∀v(wRv→[[p]]v=1).

Natural-language modals require contextually supplied modal bases and orderings: epistemic, legal, physical, practical.

Tense

Add time i to the index:

[[p]]w,i.

Past and future operators quantify over earlier or later times. Alternatives use explicit temporal variables and quantifiers.

The choice is formal and does not settle metaphysics of time.

Lambda abstraction

Lambda notation builds functions:

[[runs]]=λx.run(x).

Then:

(λx.run(x))(Ada)=run(Ada).

Abstraction supports relative clauses, quantifiers, and compositional type matching.

Generalized quantifiers

Determiner phrases can denote sets of sets:

[[every student]]=λP.∀x(student(x)→P(x)).

Applied to "runs":

∀x(student(x)→run(x)).

This treats noun phrases uniformly without making "every student" name an object.

Attitude reports

An attitude verb can relate an individual to an intension:

[[believes]]∼λp.λx.∀w′∈Belx​(w):p(w′)=1.

Belx​(w) contains worlds compatible with x's beliefs.

This predicts logical omniscience and substitution of necessary equivalents. Fine-grained belief requires structured or impossible-world content, modes of presentation, or restricted alternatives.

Context and index

Context fixes indexicals; index evaluates content. For "I might leave":

  1. context assigns the speaker to "I";
  2. modality varies worlds;
  3. the same individual is evaluated across them.

Keeping the roles separate prevents indexicals from shifting under ordinary modality.

Ontological neutrality

Worlds and functions in a semantic model need not be concrete entities. They can be abstract indices or representational devices.

The ontology of worlds remains in Possible Worlds.

Limits

Intensional semantics collapses necessary equivalents and impossible contents. It also presupposes primitive lexical values and a relevant space of worlds.

Its achievement is substantial but bounded: precise composition across modality, time, scope, and quantification. Hyperintensionality and metasemantics require additional resources.
