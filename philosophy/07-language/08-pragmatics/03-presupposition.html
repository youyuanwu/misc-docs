Presupposition

"Ada stopped smoking" presents Ada's earlier smoking as background while asserting a change. "The king of France is bald" treats a unique king as given. "Ben knows that the door is open" presents the door's being open as settled.

These backgrounded contents are presuppositions. They tend to survive negation, questions, modality, and other embeddings; they can be accommodated when not already shared; and they project from clauses in patterns sensitive to discourse structure.

Presupposition sits across semantics and pragmatics. Semantic theories use partial truth values or definedness conditions. Pragmatic theories explain backgrounding through common ground and conversational expectations. Dynamic theories treat meaning as context update and integrate both.

Triggers

Common presupposition triggers include:

  • definite descriptions: "the editor" suggests a unique relevant editor;
  • change-of-state verbs: "stop" suggests the earlier activity;
  • factive verbs: "know," "discover," and "regret" suggest truth of the complement;
  • iteratives: "again" suggests a prior event;
  • clefts: "It was Ada who left" suggests someone left;
  • possessives: "Ben's bicycle" suggests an appropriate bicycle relation;
  • temporal clauses: "Before Ada left" suggests Ada left;
  • too/either: "Ben came too" suggests someone else came.

Triggers vary in strength and projection. The category is not perfectly uniform.

Constancy under negation

Compare:

Ada stopped smoking.

Ada did not stop smoking.

Both tend to convey that Ada smoked before. The background survives ordinary negation, unlike the main asserted content.

This constancy under negation is a classic diagnostic. It also appears under:

  • questions;
  • conditionals;
  • possibility;
  • commands.

Diagnostics can conflict. Context can suspend a presupposition, and some entailments also survive certain embeddings. Constancy is evidence rather than a complete definition.

The projection problem

A presupposition introduced inside a larger sentence sometimes becomes a presupposition of the whole:

Ada believes that Ben stopped smoking.

This can suggest that Ben smoked.

But projection can be blocked:

If Ben smoked, then he has stopped smoking.

The antecedent supplies the background, so the conditional need not presuppose that Ben smoked.

It can also be cancelled:

Ada did not stop smoking - she never smoked.

An adequate theory must predict when background content projects, is filtered, is locally accommodated, or is explicitly rejected.

Semantic presupposition

On a semantic account, a sentence has a defined truth value only if its presupposition is satisfied.

For "The F is G":

defined only if exactly one relevant F exists.

If defined, it is true iff that F is G.

This explains why both positive and negative forms retain the existence condition. It can be implemented with partial functions, a third truth value, or supervaluation.

Problems include:

  • ordinary speakers sometimes judge sentences false rather than gappy;
  • presuppositions can be accommodated;
  • context can cancel or repair them;
  • projection through complex sentences needs special composition.

Semantic accounts capture conventional triggering but require pragmatic support.

Pragmatic presupposition

Stalnaker treats presupposition as what a speaker takes for granted in a conversation. The common ground is the set of propositions mutually accepted for current purposes.

To presuppose p is, roughly, to behave as though p belongs to that common ground. An utterance requiring p is felicitous when interlocutors can treat it as settled.

Advantages:

  • accommodation is natural;
  • backgrounding is tied to discourse purpose;
  • speaker presupposition can diverge from sentence triggers;
  • cancellation reflects explicit management of common ground.

The theory must still explain why particular lexical items regularly impose particular requirements. Purely general pragmatics may underdescribe trigger-specific behaviour.

Accommodation

If a speaker says:

I have to pick up my sister.

without previously mentioning a sister, listeners often add that the speaker has a sister to the common ground. This is accommodation.

Accommodation is constrained:

  • the assumption should be plausible or uncontroversial enough;
  • it should support interpretation;
  • it should not conflict with established information;
  • local accommodation may be preferred under embedding;
  • socially or epistemically costly assumptions can be challenged.

Listeners can refuse:

Wait - I did not know you had a sister.

Accommodation therefore updates conversational background without turning presupposition into unchallengeable truth.

Dynamic semantics

Dynamic semantics treats sentence meaning as a function from information states to information states:

[[s]]:C↦C′.

A presupposition is a condition on the input context. Assertion adds at-issue content to produce the output.

This approach handles:

  • discourse anaphora;
  • projection;
  • conditional filtering;
  • accommodation;
  • context change.

It blurs a simple semantics-pragmatics division: context update is formally encoded in semantics, while actual accommodation and relevance remain pragmatic.

Local accommodation

Sometimes a presupposition is accommodated only within an embedded environment:

If Ada has a bicycle, she will regret that it is outside.

The conditional need not globally presuppose that Ada has a bicycle. The information can be added locally while evaluating the consequent under the antecedent.

Local accommodation prevents unwanted global commitment but can produce readings that feel marked. Preference among global, local, and no accommodation depends on coherence, informativeness, and processing.

Presupposition versus entailment

"Ada stopped smoking" entails, or at least strongly implies, prior smoking on many analyses. Why call it presupposed rather than asserted?

Differences include:

  • projection under negation;
  • background status;
  • resistance to direct denial;
  • lower prominence in answers to the current question;
  • characteristic accommodation.

At-issue content and presupposed content can both affect truth conditions. The distinction concerns discourse role and projection, not simply whether one is truth-conditional.

Presupposition versus implicature

Both can be cancellable and context-sensitive.

Conversational implicature is calculated from what was said and conversational expectations. Presupposition is more tightly associated with triggers and background requirements.

Compare:

Some students passed.

The not all inference is readily cancelled.

Ada stopped smoking.

The prior-smoking content is harder to remove without correction or metalinguistic repair.

The difference is graded in some cases. Repeated pragmatic inferences can become conventionalized.

Factive predicates

"Ada knows that p" presupposes or entails p:

Ada knows that the door is open.

The speaker normally commits to the door being open. Under negation:

Ada does not know that the door is open.

the commitment tends to persist.

Some factives permit contextual suspension:

If the door is not open, Ada does not know that it is.

Theories debate whether factivity is lexical entailment with pragmatic projection, semantic presupposition, or a mixed phenomenon.

Presupposition and disagreement

Backgrounding can make a claim harder to contest because denial of the main assertion leaves the presupposition untouched. Interlocutors can respond through:

  • explicit rejection;
  • clarification;
  • presupposition denial;
  • alternative framing;
  • refusal to accommodate.

This pragmatic effect does not make every presupposition manipulative. Efficient conversation requires shared background. The same mechanism can be exploited when controversial content is presented as settled.

The general political development remains outside this section; the linguistic point is how background content enters and can be challenged.

Assessment

No single account explains every aspect:

AccountStrengthPressure
Semantic/partialtrigger stability and definednessaccommodation and contextual repair
Pragmatic/common grounddiscourse use and cancellationlexical regularity
Dynamicprojection and context updateformal complexity and empirical choice
Hybriddistributes trigger, projection, and accommodationboundary and redundancy

A hybrid is natural:

  1. lexical and constructional properties introduce requirements;
  2. compositional rules project or filter them;
  3. discourse context determines satisfaction;
  4. pragmatic reasoning manages accommodation and challenge.

Presupposition is therefore not evidence that semantics and pragmatics cannot be distinguished. It is evidence that they interact at multiple stages.
