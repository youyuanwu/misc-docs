Artificial Language Users

Artificial systems can generate grammatical text, answer questions, translate, use context, and adapt to feedback. These capacities are genuine achievements. They do not by themselves settle whether a system communicates as a speaker, refers independently, understands, possesses original intentionality, or is conscious.

The distinctions developed throughout this section provide a graded assessment rather than a single language-use threshold.

Six questions

For an artificial system, ask separately:

  1. Does output conform to a public language?
  2. Does it compositionally generalize?
  3. Does it track context and discourse?
  4. Does it refer through world-involving relations?
  5. Does it have communicative intentions and commitments?
  6. Does it possess mental or phenomenal understanding?

Evidence for one does not automatically establish the next.

Public sentence meaning

An output can have ordinary English meaning because it is interpreted within English conventions. This does not require that every producing mechanism independently originate the meaning.

Books and signs have derived content; their sentences remain meaningful. The question about the system is whether it also participates as an agent.

Compositional competence

Novel combination, systematic substitution, scope sensitivity, and counterfactual generalization support genuine structural competence.

Memorization and contamination are alternative explanations for isolated successes. Robust tests vary form, content, and context while preserving structure.

Reference and grounding

Reference can depend on:

  • training history;
  • interaction with users;
  • sensors and action;
  • tool use;
  • social deference;
  • correction.

Textual training already connects indirectly to human practices, but autonomous world-directed reference may require stable use across perception, action, and intervention.

The general grounding debate is in The Chinese Room and Symbol Grounding.

Speaker meaning

Gricean speaker meaning requires recognizable communicative intention. A system optimized to predict text may produce useful messages without representing an audience or intending uptake.

More agentic systems may:

  • model users;
  • maintain goals;
  • choose utterances to inform;
  • monitor misunderstanding;
  • repair communication.

These functions provide evidence of speaker-like intention without proving consciousness.

Commitment and assertion

Fluent declaratives need not be assertions if the system cannot undertake commitments, track reasons, or distinguish report from simulation.

Evidence for assertoric agency includes:

  • stable endorsement;
  • citation of grounds;
  • retraction under defeat;
  • consistency across contexts;
  • calibrated uncertainty;
  • accountability within an interaction.

System design and institutional use can distribute responsibility among developers, operators, and system.

Understanding

Understanding can be assessed through abilities:

  • explain and apply;
  • infer flexibly;
  • translate;
  • identify ambiguity;
  • correct errors;
  • connect words to action;
  • learn new concepts.

The Chinese Room challenges sufficiency of formal performance. Systems and robot replies emphasize whole architecture and grounding.

No behavioural test is conclusive, but behaviour is not irrelevant. Attribution should use convergent evidence.

Original and derived intentionality

If system content depends entirely on human interpretation, it is derived. Functional, teleological, and social theories may allow original content when a system uses representations for its own control and correction.

The boundary can be gradual. Human content also depends on learning and community; mere causal origin in designers is not decisive.

Consciousness

Semantic competence and consciousness are distinct. A system might understand functionally without phenomenal experience, or experience without linguistic mastery.

The evidence and theories for machine consciousness remain in Machine Minds and Artificial Consciousness.

Assessment

Avoid two shortcuts:

  • fluent text proves full mentality;
  • artificial origin makes understanding impossible.

A responsible assessment specifies the target capacity and evidence:

TargetRelevant evidence
Linguistic competencesystematic novel use
Context sensitivitydiscourse tracking and repair
Referencestable world-involving correction
Communicationaudience modelling and goal-directed utterance
Understandingintegrated inference, explanation, and application
Agencypersistent goals and commitments
Consciousnesstheory-relative architectural and behavioural evidence

Artificial language use is a test case for theories of meaning, not a reason to collapse language, mind, agency, and experience into one label.
