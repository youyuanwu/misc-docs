Rule-Following and Private Language

Rules appear to determine indefinitely many correct applications, yet any finite history is compatible with many continuations. Interpreting a rule through another sign threatens regress. A purely private sign appears to lack a distinction between being correct and merely seeming correct.

Wittgenstein's rule-following discussion locates meaning in practice, training, agreement, and criteria rather than in a self-interpreting mental item. This page focuses on interpretation and public standards. The sceptical quus argument and competing solutions are developed in Meaning Scepticism and Rule-Following.

The sensation diary, beetle example, first-person authority, and competing readings of the private-language argument are developed in Private Language and the Private-Language Argument.

The interpretation regress

A signpost points right. Another interpretation could assign it a deviant rule. Adding a written instruction merely introduces another sign needing interpretation.

If application always depends on a further interpretation:

Rule1​→Interpretation1​→Interpretation2​→⋯

guidance never begins.

The regress stops in a practical capacity to go on, exhibited in trained use rather than a final inner sentence.

Following versus conforming

An object can accidentally move in conformity with a rule without following it. A speaker follows a rule when performance belongs to a practice of:

  • training;
  • correction;
  • reasons;
  • repeatability;
  • distinction between success and error.

This does not require conscious rule consultation. Most linguistic rules are followed tacitly.

Agreement in judgments

Shared language depends not only on agreement in definitions but on broad agreement in applications and reactions.

Such agreement is not a vote making every judgment true. It is a background condition for correction and disagreement. If every application and standard were contested, there would be no stable practice within which a dispute was identifiable.

Criteria

A criterion is a publicly usable basis for applying a concept, not merely inductive evidence for a hidden state.

Pain behaviour and avowal provide criteria for pain language without making pain identical to behaviour. Criteria connect concept possession to shared practices while allowing deception, suppression, and exceptional cases.

This avoids both private definition and simple behaviourism.

Private ostension

Imagine a diarist marking "S" whenever a private sensation occurs. They attend inwardly and intend to use "S" for that sensation type.

On later occasions, whatever seems to match counts as matching. There is no independent distinction between:

  • remembering the rule correctly;
  • merely seeming to remember correctly.

The worry is not that others cannot verify the use. The diarist lacks a standard of correctness even for themselves if the sign's identity rests solely on present impression.

Privacy of experience

The argument does not deny private experience. It denies that private objects alone can ground a language in principle inaccessible to public criteria.

Public pain language can concern first-person experience because it is learned within patterns of injury, avowal, comfort, and action.

The philosophy-of-mind treatment of self-knowledge remains in Self-Knowledge and Introspection.

Solitary speakers

A solitary person can preserve records, compare present action with past commitments, and correct themselves. Actual company at every use is unnecessary.

The stronger requirement is publicness in principle: stable criteria and repeatable standards not exhausted by current seeming.

Whether a permanently isolated agent could invent such standards tests how literally community should enter the theory.

Normativity

Practice must preserve error. If whatever a community does defines correctness, collective error is impossible.

Public accounts can appeal to:

  • rules implicit in training;
  • inferential commitments;
  • environmental success;
  • established procedures;
  • counterfactual correction.

Community sustains norms without being infallible. The source and force of those norms are examined in The Normativity of Meaning.

Interpretation and understanding

Understanding is manifested in flexible application, explanation, correction, and coordination. No unique inner item must accompany every correct use.

This makes interpretation practical and embodied. It also warns against treating formal rules as self-applying: a grammar or semantic theory describes standards only within practices that connect its symbols to use.

Assessment

Rule-following considerations establish:

  1. interpretations cannot determine their own application indefinitely;
  2. finite behaviour does not enumerate an unbounded rule;
  3. correctness requires a standard beyond current inclination;
  4. linguistic standards are sustained in repeatable practices;
  5. private experience does not by itself fix private language.

They do not show that majority practice makes truth, that experience is public, or that formal rules are dispensable. They identify the practical background through which rules guide at all.
