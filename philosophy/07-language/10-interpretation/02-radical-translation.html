Radical Translation

Quine's radical translation imagines a field linguist confronting a wholly unknown language without bilingual speakers or prior dictionaries. The linguist observes utterances, sensory circumstances, assent, dissent, and behaviour, then constructs a translation manual.

The case asks how much linguistic evidence determines. Quine argues that multiple incompatible manuals can fit all possible behavioural evidence. Translation is not merely uncertain; on his strongest conclusion it is indeterminate.

"Gavagai"

A native speaker says "Gavagai" when a rabbit appears. Translating it as "Rabbit" is natural, but the stimulus is equally associated with:

  • undetached rabbit part;
  • rabbit stage;
  • rabbit fusion;
  • manifestation of rabbithood.

Pointing does not decide among them because ostension itself requires criteria of identity and individuation.

Stimulus meaning

Quine begins with dispositions to assent or dissent under sensory stimulation. Stimulus meaning records stimulation patterns prompting those responses.

Observation sentences provide relatively stable entry points. The linguist then identifies logical particles and theoretical connections across the language.

Stimulus meaning constrains whole-sentence translation without uniquely determining reference of component expressions.

Analytical hypotheses

To translate beyond observation, the linguist adopts analytical hypotheses:

  • how words segment;
  • which expressions are predicates or quantifiers;
  • what objects variables range over;
  • how identity works;
  • how constructions compose.

Different hypothesis systems can preserve all behavioural predictions while assigning different structures and ontologies.

Translation is assessed globally. No single word-by-word fact independently settles the manual.

Indeterminacy of translation

The strong thesis says:

There can be multiple translation manuals, incompatible in assigned meanings, that fit the totality of possible behavioural evidence equally well.

This differs from ordinary lack of evidence. Quine denies a hidden semantic fact that one manual alone captures if no empirical difference distinguishes them.

The conclusion follows from Quine's naturalism and rejection of meanings as additional entities. A realist can accept evidential equivalence while positing causal, intentional, or normative facts beyond behaviour.

Inscrutability of reference

Even after sentence translation is fixed, terms can be systematically reinterpreted through proxy functions while preserving truth. Reference is less determinate than whole-sentence use.

This is developed in Reference Indeterminacy.

Translation and theory

Quine compares translation with scientific theory. Both face underdetermination by evidence and are evaluated through simplicity, conservatism, and total explanatory fit.

But indeterminacy is intended to be deeper: rival physical theories may disagree about facts, while rival manuals can be alternative coordinate systems without a further fact of correct translation.

Critics dispute whether this contrast can be maintained.

Charity and logical particles

The linguist translates connectives in ways making speakers broadly logical. Persistent apparent contradiction is more likely evidence of mistranslation than discovery that a community accepts everything.

This principle is related to Davidsonian charity, though Quine remains more behaviourist and sceptical. Translating logic itself may involve choices among inferential systems, not merely imposing classical logic.

The move to indeterminacy requires a metaphysical premise about which facts are acceptable. Behavioural equivalence alone does not force it.

Assessment

Radical translation shows that interpretation depends on global theoretical choices about segmentation, logic, ontology, and reference. It undermines a picture where each word carries an independently observable meaning.

Its sceptical conclusion remains contested. Causal contact, shared practice, speaker intention, naturalness, and rationality may constrain translation beyond stimulus meaning. The enduring lesson is methodological: a translation manual is a structured hypothesis, not a list read directly from behaviour.
